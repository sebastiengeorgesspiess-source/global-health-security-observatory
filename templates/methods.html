Global Pathogen & Health Security Observatory

A reproducible exploratory evidence system combining official outbreak notices, country health indicators and climate context without producing clinical advice, causal claims or a composite risk ranking.

1 · PURPOSE AND SCOPE

The Observatory supports country comparison and hypothesis generation. Its unit of analysis is predominantly the country or territory. It is not a diagnostic, forecasting or operational emergency-response system.

Primary questions

  • Which official public-health events have recently been assessed by WHO?
  • What protection, capacity, exposure and vulnerability context is available?
  • How complete and current is the evidence for a selected country?

2 · ANALYTIC FRAME

Evidence remains separated across five dimensions: verified threat signal, exposure, vulnerability, capacity and evidence confidence. No weighted total is calculated because compensatory weighting could conceal a severe weakness and imply unsupported precision.

Evidence confidence measures availability and freshness—not country performance, transparency or safety.

4 · PROCESSING

  1. Retrieve official country metadata and country-year series.
  2. Exclude aggregates and retain missing observations.
  3. Store the latest non-missing observation and the 2010–2025 timeline.
  4. Round displayed values to two decimals without changing source units.
  5. Join the versioned heat artifact by ISO3 code.
  6. Publish only if the release gate contains at least 180 country profiles and one WHO notice.
  7. Replace the cache atomically; a failed refresh preserves the last verified release.

5 · UNCERTAINTY AND MISSINGNESS

Source-specific model uncertainty is retained where supplied, including P10–P90 for the climate layer. WDI point estimates do not provide harmonised uncertainty intervals through this interface. The Observatory therefore does not invent them.

“No data”, “not applicable” and “zero” are distinct states. Only source-reported numerical zero is displayed as zero.

6 · LIMITATIONS

  • Country averages conceal subnational inequality.
  • Reference years differ between indicators.
  • Reporting capacity affects observed event visibility.
  • Ecological comparison cannot establish individual-level causation.
  • Spatial overlap cannot demonstrate an exposure–outcome relationship.
  • Tourism arrivals are incomplete mobility proxies.
  • WOAH animal events, pathogen genomics and wastewater surveillance are not yet integrated.

7 · ETHICS AND RESPONSIBLE USE

The interface uses aggregate public data and avoids person-level records. It must not be used to stigmatise countries or populations. Reporting gaps are shown as uncertainty, not failure. The interpretation follows WHO principles for ethical public-health surveillance.

8 · REPRODUCIBILITY AND CITATION

Every release exposes machine-readable data, country CSV/JSON profiles, a BibTeX record, release metadata and an evidence registry containing the SHA-256 hash of the generated artifact. Automated refresh runs every six hours.

9 · REPORTING STANDARD

This statement is structured around the transparency goals of the WHO-convened GATHER guidance: input data, data processing, modelled versus observed classification, missingness, limitations and reproducible access are disclosed. It is not a claim of formal GATHER certification or peer review.
